# Fronts of a grown surface that meet

Date: 18 November 2026. Written on `feat/underground-ceiling`, after the commit tagged `ceiling-convex-v1`, which keeps the surface as it was for convex spaces. Not committed.

## Change impact

**Request.** An excavation of chambers joined by tunnels, with a loop among them, grown by `growChamber`, showed thin sheets of rock across the mouths of its tunnels. The user asked to fix the growth, and to freeze the version before it for convex spaces first.

**Cause.** Where two fronts of the surface come face to face within 0.9 edge length, both stop (`contact`). Two causes, measured on that excavation (5 chambers, 5 tunnels, edges of 2 m): fronts on either side of a wedge of rock, where a tunnel opens into a chamber, stopped each other through the rock; and fronts that met across the volume, around the neck of a tunnel that opens into a larger room, and where the loop of tunnels closes. A surface that keeps the topology of a sphere cannot fill a loop.

**Components.** `grown-surface.ts`, `chamber.ts`, `rock-face.ts` and two new modules, `fuse.ts` and `texel-fusion.ts`, of `@spacexr/ceiling`. `IGrowthReport` gains `fusion`; `IGrownSurfaceOptions` and `IGrowChamberOptions` gain `fuse`; `GrownSurface` gains `meetingPoints` and the result of `growChamber` gains `meetings`; `growChamber` and `growSurface` take the mesh before the report.

**Changes.**

1. Behaviour change: two fronts stop each other only when the segment between them lies in the volume, asked at four points. Fronts with rock between them each go on to their own wall. On the excavation, contacts went from 756 to 566 vertices; the thin walls, counted as vertices within 0.8 m of a vertex facing the other way, from 2 864 to 2 409. Surfaces of volumes without such wedges are unchanged.
2. New, off by default: `fuse: true` makes `GrownSurface.mesh` call `fuseContacts` (`fuse.ts`). The growth keeps the pair of vertices of each meeting. From each pair, two regions of faces grow together, one on each sheet, a face joining its region only when a face of the other faces it within 1.2 edge length with the volume between them. Two regions apart leave a hole each, sewn to each other by a strip; two regions that touch are one sheet folded on itself, and leave one slit, zipped from tip to tip. A fusion that would not leave a closed surface is undone.
3. New, a prototype, not called by the growth: `fuseByTexels` (`texel-fusion.ts`), after a proposal of the user. Round the places where fronts met, a box is cut into texels of half the edge length; a texel is rock outside the volume, filled inside the surface, and the filling goes on through the volume, which closes the gap between the fronts. In the box the surface is rebuilt on the grid as surface nets, a vertex only ever at a point of the grid, so two fronts that reach the same point are one vertex; the rebuilt part is sewn to the rest of the surface where the box ends. `GrownSurface.meetingPoints` gives it the places, and `growChamber` returns them as `meetings`. What the excavation asked of it, each found by a box that failed or by faces left off the wall:
    - The filled texels are made well composed before the surface is drawn from them: where two touch only along an edge or at a corner, a texel of rock beside them is taken as filled. Without it, at the tip of a wedge of rock thinner than a texel, between a tunnel and the chamber it opens into, the surface on the grid had an edge with four faces.
    - The faces replaced are those with a vertex within the rebuilt part or within half a texel of it, and those round a vertex where the hole would touch itself. Cut by the middle of the faces, the faces left reached into the rebuilt part and the seam folded back on itself.
    - The two loops of a seam are walked at the same pace, neither more than 6 % of its length ahead: by the shorter diagonal alone, the seam had triangles up to 13 m long.
    - Every vertex of the rebuilt part, those of its side too, is smoothed with its neighbours through the seam, then moved out along its normal to the wall of the volume, as the vertices of the growth are: a point of the grid stands up to half a texel short of the wall.
    - A box whose rebuilt part does not fit is tried again an edge length larger, up to four times: where its side passes through such a wedge, the grid and the surface do not count the same loops.
4. Behaviour change of `buildRockFace`: only the edges longer than the edge length are split, each triangle in two, three or four by its edges split. It split every triangle in four while any edge was too long, so one long edge refined the whole mesh once more. A mesh whose edges are all too long is refined as before.

**Measured on the excavation** (5 chambers, 5 tunnels with a loop, edges of 2 m, 55 914 triangles grown, 566 contacts), by its build script in `spacexr-rendering/examples/babylon-excavation`, one run on one machine. The faces of the gaps left by the growth, those with the volume half a metre out of them and the surface not there: 1 062 faces, 1 025 m². `fuseByTexels`: 6 boxes, 6 fused, one of them at its second try, 780 texels of gap filled, in 1.7 s; 62 986 triangles, closed, every edge once in each direction, one hole through, the loop of tunnels; longest edge 3.2 m. Faces of gaps left: one by the count, of 0.74 m², which is a face of the wall on a wedge of rock 0.4 m thick, 54 m from any meeting of fronts: none left by the fusion. Looked at in a browser where the walls were, the rock is continuous and the mouth where the loop closes is open.

**Known limits.** `fuseContacts` fills a ring: a slab around a pillar becomes a closed torus. On the excavation it fused 11 walls and left 7, among them the largest (about 300 faces a side, whose hole pinches). Hence off by default; `fuseByTexels` does what it does not, and can take its place. `fuseByTexels` is not called by the growth yet: a caller runs it on the surface and its meetings. It was tried on three volumes: the slab around a pillar, a loop of two rooms and two tunnels, and the excavation. A box that does not fit after its tries is left as it was, its walls with it, and the report says why. In a box the surface has edges of a texel, half the edge length of the rest. The tip of a wedge of rock thinner than a texel is worn by less than a texel. Both fusions take features of the volume thinner than the edge length for walls.

**Costs.** The test of the segment asks the volume at most four more times per vertex that meets another front: `COST_NOT_ISOLATED`. `fuseByTexels` asks the volume once per texel of its boxes, about 120 000 texels on the excavation, and a few times per vertex of the rebuilt parts: 1.7 s there against 11 s of growth, 33 to 53 ms on the slab around a pillar.

**Rationale.** Four points along the segment and a reach of 1.2 edge length for the walls are `RATIONALE_UNKNOWN`: first values tried. So are, in `fuseByTexels`, the texel of half the edge length, the box three edge lengths round the meetings, the guard of half a texel, the four passes of smoothing, the reach of a texel and a half to the wall and the four tries of a box: each is the first value that passed on the excavation.

## Verification

`grown-surface.test.ts`: the slab around a pillar, fused (closed, one hole through, more volume than back to back) and not fused (closed, topology of a sphere, as before); the report of a seed has `fusion: null`. `texel-fusion.test.ts`: the same slab fused on texels, closed, each edge once in each direction, one hole through, more volume than before; a loop of two rooms joined by two tunnels, a sphere before and a closed surface with one hole through after, no box left, every vertex in the volume; a ball, whose fronts never meet, returned as it was given. `rock-face.test.ts`: a stretched octahedron whose long edges alone are split. 81 tests of the package pass, with typecheck and lint. The two tests of the chamber of the Studio of geomodels-explorer, which builds on this package, pass too.
